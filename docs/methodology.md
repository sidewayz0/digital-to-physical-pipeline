# Research Methodology

[Home](./) · [Investigations](cases/) · [Source documentation](references/)

## Research purpose and scope

**The Digital-to-Physical Pipeline** examines publicly documented Russian intelligence recruitment, reconnaissance, and hybrid operations in Europe. The initial case-study focus is Poland and Lithuania, with cross-case comparisons reserved for when there is sufficient independently evaluated evidence.

The central concern is not simply whether an incident occurred, but what public evidence can establish about its participants, tasking, recruitment pathways, preparation, possible intelligence connections, and implications.

## Source hierarchy

The research prioritizes material according to its proximity to the underlying facts, evidentiary transparency, and ability to corroborate specific claims:

1. **Primary judicial and prosecutorial records:** judgments, indictments, prosecutor releases, and officially available case records.
2. **Official intelligence and government assessments:** named security services, joint advisories, and formal attribution statements.
3. **Investigative journalism and contemporaneous reporting:** especially reporting with original interviews, documents, or on-the-record sources.
4. **Court and proceeding coverage:** used with care to distinguish statements in court, charges, testimony, findings, and final judgments.
5. **Expert and research literature:** contextual analysis by specialist institutes, scholars, and nongovernmental organizations.

This is a priority order, not a substitute for source-by-source evaluation: a detailed original investigation may support a particular fact more directly than a broad official summary.

## Claims and evidence

Each material claim should be traceable to one or more sources. Published analysis will, where practicable, distinguish:

- **Established fact:** supported by an authoritative primary record or sufficiently corroborated documentation.
- **Reported allegation:** asserted in a source but not independently established by the available record.
- **Analyst assessment:** an inference from identified evidence, labeled with an appropriate confidence statement and limitations.
- **Unresolved question:** a material issue for which the reviewed records remain incomplete, contradictory, or unavailable.

Multiple publications repeating the same original reporting should not be counted as independent corroboration. The research will identify important dependencies and discrepancies where known.

## Chronology and attribution

Incident dates, dates when events were disclosed, dates of judicial action, and dates sources were published or accessed are treated separately. Attribution to a state institution, intelligence service, intermediary, or participant will not be presented as established merely because an event resembles previously reported tradecraft.

Convictions, charges, prosecutorial assertions, official intelligence statements, and independent analytical conclusions have different evidentiary meanings and will be labeled accordingly.

## Confidence and limitations

Assessments should state their evidentiary basis, significant alternatives, information gaps, and confidence levels where relevant. Confidence reflects the quality and convergence of evidence, not the perceived severity of a threat.

If full court records or other material primary records have not been obtained or reviewed, that limitation will remain visible in the publication. Subsequent evidence may warrant revision, and revisions should be documented through version history and dated updates.

## Publication and source protection

This public repository is a curated publication, **not a mirror of the underlying private research vault**. Internal notes, administrative records, unpublished correspondence, and personal information are not published by default.

Published reports should cite public source URLs, publication dates, access dates when useful, and archived references where lawful and available. Quotes, images, and reproduced documents should respect licensing and copyright restrictions.
